\begin{abstract}
We formalize a restricted class of fixed response strategies for finite gravity
Connect Four and prove a structural classification by isolated column
advancement. On an odd-width, odd-height board after one opening disc, this
classification yields an exact counting formula. We then prove that on every
nine-column board of odd height at least five, every universally legal fixed
response matching for the first player admits a second-player counterattack
within sixteen plies. Separately, we give a sufficient, certificate-producing
midgame theorem based on vertical pairs and currently playable cross-pairs.
An explicit reachable draw and an eight-node winning endgame certificate
illustrate the distinction between a position proof and an opening solution.
The associated computation completed a 27-ply finite-horizon search of the
empty $9\times9$ board, but did not determine its full-game value. The present
note therefore does not claim a weak or ultra-weak solution of that board,
or priority for the underlying pairing ideas.
\end{abstract}

\section[Game and unresolved objective]{Game, notation, and the unresolved objective}\label{sec:game}
Let
\[
 \B_{w,h}=\{(c,r):1\le c\le w,\ 1\le r\le h\},
\]
with row $1$ at the bottom. A gravity position has occupied cells
$\{(c,1),\ldots,(c,a_c)\}$ in column $c$, for heights $0\le a_c\le h$.
A move in a nonfull column $c$ occupies $(c,a_c+1)$. The players alternate,
with $\Pone$ first. A player wins immediately on obtaining four of its discs
in a horizontal, vertical, or diagonal line. Play ends at the first win,
or with a draw when all cells are occupied without a win. There are no
passes, removals, wraparound columns, or mis\`ere rules.

Let $\W_{w,h}$ be the family of four-cell winning sets. For $w,h\ge4$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lines}
 |\W_{w,h}|=h(w-3)+w(h-3)+2(w-3)(h-3).
\end{equation}
Thus $|\W_{9,9}|=54+54+36+36=180$. Equation~\eqref{eq:lines} follows by
choosing the leftmost cell of a horizontal four, the bottom cell of a
vertical four, and the bottom endpoint of either diagonal orientation.

For a legal nonterminal position $S$, define $\Val(S)\in\{-1,0,1\}$ from the
perspective of the player to move: loss, draw, and win, respectively, under
optimal play. At a terminal position immediately after an opponent's win,
set $\Val=-1$; at a terminal draw, set $\Val=0$. Backward induction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimax}
 \Val(S)=\max_{c\in L(S)}\{-\Val(S_c)\},
\end{equation}
where $L(S)$ is the set of nonfull columns and $S_c$ is the position after
playing $c$. This is a finite recursion because each move consumes a cell.

\begin{definition}
A \emph{weak solution} determines the empty-board value and supplies a
strategy attaining that value against every legal opposing continuation.
An \emph{ultra-weak solution}, in the terminology used here, establishes the
empty-board value without necessarily constructing an executable strategy.
\end{definition}

Neither objective follows from a favorable principal variation, an unbeaten
self-play record, a nonzero heuristic score, or a timeout. In particular,
our restricted obstruction below is not a second-player solution of the
full game. The strategic-rule tradition for Connect Four includes Allis's
Claimeven and Baseinverse ideas~\cite{allis}; our pairing formalization is
presented as a self-contained research development, without a literature
priority claim.

\section[Universally legal response matchings]{Universally legal fixed response matchings}\label{sec:classification}
The classification concerns gravity and occupancy only. For its definition,
we temporarily ignore the rule stopping play after four in a row.

\begin{definition}\label{def:ul}
Fix an initial gravity occupancy $S$ with an even number of empty cells
$\E(S)$. A \emph{response matching} $M$ is a perfect matching of $\E(S)$.
An initiator chooses any legal empty cell $x$; the responder must immediately
occupy its mate $M(x)$. A \emph{completed-round state} is a state reached
from $S$ after finitely many such legal two-cell rounds.

The matching is \emph{universally legal}, abbreviated $\UL$, if at every
completed-round state, every legal initiating move has an immediately legal
mate response. This requirement is imposed even on occupancies that would
already have ended a Connect Four game.
\end{definition}

This last qualification is substantial. A matching with an illegal response
only after the responder has already won is not necessarily $\UL$.
Adaptive matchings and fallback moves are also outside this definition.

A \emph{front} of a nonfull column is its lowest empty cell. For a column
with $r_i=h-a_i$ empty cells, enumerate its empty cells from bottom to top
as $u_{i,1},\ldots,u_{i,r_i}$.

\begin{lemma}[Initial fronts]\label{lem:fronts}
At the start of a universally legal response process, the mate of each
front is either the cell immediately above it in the same column, or the
front of a different column.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $x$ be a front. Before the initiating move at $x$, the only legal cells
are the current fronts. After that move, the only newly legal cell is the
cell immediately above $x$, if it exists. Its mate must be one of these
legal cells. A different cell higher in another column remains unsupported.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Isolated advancement]\label{lem:isolation}
Suppose that at a completed-round state the initial fronts of a collection
of columns are matched internally in that collection, and all other columns
are kept frozen.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If a column's front is matched vertically to the next cell, all its
remaining cells are paired into consecutive vertical dominoes.
\item If the fronts of two columns are matched to each other, their
cross-column pairs form a common initial prefix of their remaining cells.
After the prefix ends, all remaining pairs in each column are consecutive
vertical dominoes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For (i), consume the front's vertical pair while leaving every other
column unchanged. By \cref{lem:fronts}, each frozen front was initially matched either to
another initial front or to its own immediate successor. In particular,
none was matched to a cell newly exposed in the advancing column. A cell higher in a frozen column is not playable. By universal
legality, the newly exposed front must therefore be paired vertically to
the cell directly above it. Repeating the same argument advances this
column alone to completion. Its remaining cell count must be even.

For (ii), consume the cross-pair and keep all other columns frozen. The
new fronts, when both exist, cannot be matched to any outside front:
those fronts have their own fixed mates. They cannot be matched to any
higher outside cell, because it is unsupported. By
\cref{lem:fronts}, either the two new fronts are paired to each other,
or at least one of them is paired vertically.

If one front is paired vertically, the other cannot be paired to either
cell of that vertical pair, since both mates are already fixed. It also
cannot be paired to a higher cell in that column: that cell is not playable
at the present state, whereas the other front can be initiated immediately.
It must therefore be paired vertically as well. Part (i), now advancing
either column with every other column frozen, forces vertical pairs above
that point in both columns. If instead a column is exhausted at the end
of a cross-pair, the other column, if nonempty, is forced to finish
vertically by the same isolated-advancement argument. Consequently the
cross-pairs form a prefix and cannot resume after a vertical split.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{14\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Structure of universally legal response matchings]\label{thm:structure}
For an arbitrary initial gravity occupancy, a response matching is
universally legal if and only if the nonempty columns admit a partition
into components of the following two types:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item a single column with an even number of empty cells, matched into
consecutive vertical dominoes;
\item two columns $i,j$ with $k$ cross-pairs
\[
 \{u_{i,t},u_{j,t}\},\qquad 1\le t\le k,
\]
for some $1\le k\le\min(r_i,r_j)$, followed by consecutive vertical
dominoes in each column. Necessarily $r_i-k$ and $r_j-k$ are even.
\end{enumerate}
The component partition and each nonempty cross-prefix are determined by
the matching.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:fronts}, the initial fronts are paired either vertically
within their columns or reciprocally with another front. This partitions
the nonempty columns initially into singletons and two-column components.
No column belongs to two components, because a front has one mate.

Each component can be advanced on its own, leaving all other components
frozen. The argument in \cref{lem:isolation} shows that it cannot acquire a
later connection to another component: an outside front already has its
fixed mate, and every higher outside cell is unsupported when the component
is advanced alone. The same lemma supplies exactly the vertical singleton
form and the cross-prefix--then--vertical form. The parity conditions follow
because the tails consist of dominoes. Initial front mates determine the
components; the maximal consecutive cross-prefix determines $k$.

Conversely, consider a matching of the asserted form. In a singleton,
every initiating move at a domino's lower cell exposes its upper mate.
In a two-column component, each cross-pair consumes one cell in each
column, keeping the two fronts synchronized by relative depth until the
prefix ends. Both front cells of the next cross-pair are then playable.
Once the prefix is exhausted, the columns are independent vertical stacks
of dominoes. Interleaving rounds from different components changes none of
these facts. Every prescribed response is therefore legal.
\end{proof}

\subsection[Counting after one opening]{Counting after one opening disc}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:count}
Let $w=2m+1$ and $h=2n+1$. After one bottom-row opening disc in a fixed
column $q$, the number of universally legal response matchings is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
 N_{w,h}=(2m-1)!!\,(n+1)^m,
\end{equation}
with $(-1)!!=1$ when $m=0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The opening column has $2n$ empty cells; every other column has $2n+1$.
Two columns in a cross component have the same empty-count parity by
\cref{thm:structure}. Thus the unique even column cannot join an odd
column and is a vertical singleton. Each odd column must join another odd
column. There are $(2m-1)!!$ perfect matchings of these $2m$ columns.
Each paired component permits exactly the odd prefix lengths
$1,3,\ldots,2n+1$, giving $n+1$ choices independently for each of the $m$
components. The representation is unique, so the product neither omits nor
double-counts a matching.
\end{proof}

For $9\times9$, this is
\[
 N_{9,9}=7!!\,5^4=105\cdot625=65\,625
\]
for each fixed opening, or $590\,625$ configurations over all nine openings.
Reflection allows the five representative openings $q=1,\ldots,5$ to cover
the remaining four. The enumeration therefore checked
$5N_{9,9}=328\,125$ configurations. This last number is \emph{not} the
number of distinct matching orbits under reflection; at the central opening,
reflection can still identify two of the checked matchings.

\section[Counterattack obstruction]{A counterattack theorem for nine columns}\label{sec:obstruction}
The next theorem is an extension of the recorded $9\times9$ obstruction.
Its proof uses only that the height is odd and at least five.

\begin{theorem}[Restricted response obstruction]\label{thm:obstruction}
On a $9\times H$ board with odd $H\ge5$, let $\Pone$ open in any column
and thereafter respond according to a fixed universally legal perfect
matching of the remaining cells. Then $\Ptwo$ has a counterstrategy that
wins by game ply $16$, or earlier. Hence no strategy in this specified
family guarantees $\Pone$ a nonloss.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:structure,cor:count}, the opening column is vertical, and the
other eight columns form four paired components with odd cross-prefix
lengths. Write $q$ for the opening column.

\textbf{Case 1: some cross-prefix has length at least three.}
Choose one column $a$ in that component, whose partner is $b$. Player
$\Ptwo$ drops its first four discs consecutively in column $a$.
The first three prescribed replies lie in column $b$, at rows $1,2,3$.
Player $\Ptwo$ therefore wins vertically at $(a,4)$, on game ply $8$.
The cell exists since $H\ge5$. Before that move, $\Pone$ has only the
opening disc in column $q\notin\{a,b\}$ and at most three discs in column
$b$. Such a set cannot contain four collinear cells: a vertical line in
$b$ has at most three, and a nonvertical line contains at most one cell
of each column. Thus $\Pone$ cannot have won earlier.

\textbf{Case 2: every cross-prefix has length one.}
Only the bottom cells of each paired component are cross-paired; above
them, all columns have vertical pairs $(2,3),(4,5),\ldots$.
For each of the four bottom pairs, let $\Ptwo$ choose one endpoint and let
$\Pone$ receive the other as its prescribed response. We first show that
these choices can avoid a $\Pone$ bottom-row four.

Choose the four orientations uniformly and independently. An interval of
four bottom cells containing $q$ is wholly $\Pone$ with probability at
most $2^{-3}$: if two of its other cells are paired to each other the
probability is zero; otherwise it fixes three independent orientations.
An interval not containing $q$ has probability at most $2^{-4}$ by the
same reasoning. There are six four-cell intervals, at most four containing
$q$. The union bound gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:union}
 \Pr(\text{$\Pone$ has a bottom-row four})
 \le \frac4{8}+\frac2{16}=\frac58<1.
\end{equation}
Consequently a safe orientation exists.

Fix such an orientation and fill the four bottom pairs, in any order.
Player $\Pone$ cannot win during this preparation: its occupied cells
are a subset of its eventual safe bottom-row set. If $\Ptwo$ wins during
preparation, the theorem already holds. Otherwise the entire bottom row
is now full, after game ply $9$.

Player $\Ptwo$ next plays row $2$ in columns $1,2,3,4$ in that order.
Each prescribed reply is row $3$ in the same column. The fourth initiating
move completes a horizontal four for $\Ptwo$, on ply $16$.
Before that move, $\Pone$ has no row-$2$ cells, no cells above row $3$,
and at most three row-$3$ cells. Its bottom row has no four by construction.
It cannot therefore win horizontally, vertically, or diagonally before
$\Ptwo$ wins. This completes both cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact $9\times9$ enumeration split]\label{cor:split}
For five representative openings, $327\,600$ classified configurations
have a prefix of length at least three. The remaining $525$ have every
prefix length one. Their respective counterattacks terminate by plies $8$
and $16$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For each fixed opening, the all-one-prefix choices number $105$; all other
choices number $65\,625-105=65\,520$. Multiply each number by five.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Quantifier scope]
The conclusion is
\[
 \forall M\in\UL\quad\exists\ \text{a $\Ptwo$ counterstrategy against }M.
\]
It is not a statement that $\Ptwo$ beats arbitrary $\Pone$ strategies,
and it does not provide a universal counterstrategy against a hidden or
adaptively changing matching. The full-game value is not determined.
\end{remark}

\section[Certified midgame bounds]{A sufficient midgame certificate}\label{sec:oracle}
Let $C$ be the player to move and $D$ the responder in an ongoing position
$S$, with occupied sets $A_C,A_D$. Suppose $|\E(S)|$ is even. In every
column with an even number of empty cells, pair successive empty cells
vertically. In each column with an odd number, reserve its lowest empty
cell and vertically pair the cells above it. The number of reserved cells
is even, so match them in arbitrary cross-column pairs.

Denote the vertical pairs by $M_v$, the reserved-cell pairs by $M_b$, and
write
\[
 U=\{\text{upper endpoint of a pair in }M_v\},\qquad G=A_D\cup U.
\]
The reserved cells can have different absolute row coordinates; all are
currently playable, which is the fact needed for legality.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:midlegal}
The strategy in which $D$ completes the pair containing $C$'s latest move
is universally legal. Player $C$ never obtains a cell of $U$, and obtains
at most one endpoint of each pair in $M_b$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The reserved bottom cells of an unconsumed cross-pair are both playable.
Consuming the pair leaves even empty heights in its two columns. All other
rounds consume a consecutive vertical domino; only its lower cell is
playable before the round, and the initiating move exposes the upper
response. An induction on completed rounds proves legality and assigns
each vertical upper cell to $D$. Pair completion gives opposite owners to
the two cells of every cross-pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Line-cover certificate]\label{thm:cover}
With the construction above, suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cover}
 \forall L\in\W_{w,h},\qquad
 L\cap G\ne\varnothing
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \exists\{u,v\}\in M_b:\ \{u,v\}\subseteq L.
\end{equation}
Then $\Val(S)\le0$. If, in addition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:guaranteedwin}
 \exists L_D\in\W_{w,h}\quad L_D\subseteq G,
\end{equation}
then $\Val(S)=-1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix any winning line $L$ for the current player $C$. If it meets $A_D$,
that opposing disc blocks it permanently. If it meets $U$, the cell is
never owned by $C$ by \cref{lem:midlegal}; before it is filled, its being
empty already prevents completion by $C$. Otherwise
\eqref{eq:cover} supplies a cross-pair inside $L$, and $C$ cannot obtain
both endpoints. Thus no legal initiating continuation can give $C$ a win.
This proves $\Val(S)\le0$.

If \eqref{eq:guaranteedwin} holds, continue the response strategy. The
board is finite and each completed round consumes two cells. Unless $D$
has already won, play must reach full occupancy, at which point all cells
of $U$ have been obtained by $D$. Then $L_D$ is a completed $D$ line,
contradicting a terminal draw. Since $C$ cannot win, $D$ forces a win and
$\Val(S)=-1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection[Matching-cover search]{A finite matching-cover search}
Let $O$ be the reserved cells and define the remaining constraints
\[
 \mathcal R=\{L\cap O:L\in\W_{w,h},\ L\cap G=\varnothing\}.
\]
A certificate exists in this construction exactly when there is a perfect
matching of $O$ such that every $R\in\mathcal R$ contains one of its
pairs. A constraint with $|R|<2$ rules out this construction immediately.
One can branch on a constrained $R$, select two of its still-unmatched
vertices, remove constraints covered by that pair, and recurse.

This backtracking is complete for the stated construction: every full
solution must select a pair in the chosen constraint, so one branch agrees
with it. Once all constraints are covered, the unused reserved vertices
have even cardinality and may be paired arbitrarily. On a nine-column
board, $|O|$ is even and at most eight. Failure to find such a matching
establishes only failure of this sufficient certificate format, not a
winning value for $C$.

\subsection[Explicit winning endgame]{A fully explicit winning endgame}\label{sec:endgame}
Use columns $A,\ldots,I$ and write $X$ for $\Pone$, $O$ for $\Ptwo$.
After the 74-ply history in \cref{app:histories}, the board is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endboard}
\begin{array}{c|ccccccccc}
 &A&B&C&D&E&F&G&H&I\\\hline
9&X&\cdot&O&O&\cdot&X&\cdot&O&\cdot\\
8&O&O&X&O&\cdot&X&X&X&O\\
7&O&X&X&X&\cdot&O&O&O&X\\
6&X&O&X&O&\cdot&X&X&O&O\\
5&X&O&O&X&X&O&O&X&X\\
4&X&X&O&X&O&X&X&O&O\\
3&O&O&O&X&X&O&X&X&O\\
2&O&O&X&O&X&X&O&O&X\\
1&X&O&X&X&X&O&O&X&O
\end{array}
\end{equation}
Its height vector is $(9,8,9,9,5,9,8,9,8)$ and it is $\Pone$'s turn.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:endgame}
In \eqref{eq:endboard}, $\Pone$ wins within three further plies by playing
$B9$ and then responding in column $E$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The move $B9$ is legal. The only remaining nonfull columns are then
$E,G,I$. If $\Ptwo$ plays $E6$, the legal response $E7$ completes the
horizontal line $B7,C7,D7,E7$. If $\Ptwo$ instead plays $G9$ or $I9$,
the legal response $E6$ completes the diagonal $B9,C8,D7,E6$.
None of the three opposing moves is a win, as checked directly from
\eqref{eq:endboard}. These are all legal replies, so the strategy covers
every opponent continuation. The corresponding rooted certificate has
one root, one node after $B9$, three opponent-move nodes, and three winning
leaves: eight nodes in total.
\end{proof}

\section{An explicit reachable draw}\label{sec:draw}
The existence of a legal drawn game is different from the existence of a
strategy forcing a draw. It does, however, refute any argument that odd
board area by itself makes a draw impossible.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:draw}
There exists a legal drawn game of length $81$ on the $9\times9$ board.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take the 81-digit history $\sigma$ in \cref{app:histories}. Each digit
occurs nine times, so no move overfills a column. Alternating odd and even
plies gives the following final board:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccccc}
 &A&B&C&D&E&F&G&H&I\\\hline
9&X&O&X&O&O&X&O&O&O\\
8&X&O&O&O&X&O&O&O&X\\
7&O&X&X&X&O&X&X&O&O\\
6&X&X&O&X&X&X&O&X&X\\
5&O&X&X&O&O&O&X&O&X\\
4&X&O&O&O&X&X&X&O&X\\
3&O&X&X&X&O&X&O&X&O\\
2&X&X&O&O&X&X&O&X&O\\
1&X&O&X&O&X&O&X&O&O
\end{array}
\]
It contains $41$ discs of $X$ and $40$ of $O$. For an exact arithmetic
check of all winning lines, index a cell by $j=9(r-1)+(c-1)$ and encode
a color set as an integer whose $j$th bit is its occupancy. In hexadecimal,
\begin{align*}
 X&=\mathtt{25889bb7746b8ab96655},\\
 O&=\mathtt{1da7764488b94754699aa},\\
 X\mathbin{\vert}O&=2^{81}-1=\mathtt{1ffffffffffffffffffff}.
\end{align*}
For a positive direction shift $d$, let $Q_d$ mark all valid starting
cells of a four in that direction. The exact masks are
\[
\begin{array}{ccl}
 d&\text{direction}&Q_d\ \text{(hexadecimal)}\\\hline
 1&\text{horizontal}&\mathtt{3f1f8fc7e3f1f8fc7e3f}\\
 9&\text{vertical}&\mathtt{3fffffffffffff}\\
10&\text{rising right}&\mathtt{7e3f1f8fc7e3f}\\
 8&\text{rising left}&\mathtt{3f1f8fc7e3f1f8}
\end{array}
\]
Equivalently, the masks have the exact formulas
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q_1&=63\sum_{r=0}^{8}2^{9r}, & Q_9&=2^{54}-1,\\
 Q_{10}&=63\sum_{r=0}^{5}2^{9r}, & Q_8&=504\sum_{r=0}^{5}2^{9r}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Direct integer calculation gives, for each $d\in\{1,9,10,8\}$ and each
$Z\in\{X,O\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drawbits}
 Q_d\AND Z\AND(Z\mathbin{\gg}d)
       \AND(Z\mathbin{\gg}2d)\AND(Z\mathbin{\gg}3d)=0.
\end{equation}
Here $\AND$ and $\gg$ denote bitwise AND and right shift. The starting
masks exclude row wraparound, so \eqref{eq:drawbits} checks exactly the
180 winning sets. Neither color has four on the final board. Since a
color's occupied set only grows, no earlier prefix could have contained
four either. The sequence is therefore legal up to its full-board draw.
\end{proof}

\section[Finite-horizon computation]{What finite-horizon computation can establish}\label{sec:horizon}
Define $\Val_d(S)$ by using the same terminal values as $\Val$ and the
recursion \eqref{eq:minimax}, but assigning value zero to every nonterminal
position at depth zero. A depth-zero cutoff is an unresolved leaf, not an
assertion that the full game is drawn.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:horizon}
If an exact finite-horizon computation gives $\Val_d(S)=1$, then
$\Val(S)=1$. If it gives $\Val_d(S)=-1$, then $\Val(S)=-1$.
If $\Val_d(S)=0$, neither player has a strategy forcing its own win within
$d$ additional plies, but the full-game value can still be any of
$-1,0,1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The nonzero implications follow by induction on $d$. A positive value
has a child of negative value; by induction the opponent loses in that
child. A negative value has every child positive; by induction the
opponent wins after every legal choice. The terminal cases are immediate.
Equivalently, in the truncated game a nonzero value is certified by a
strategy subtree all of whose relevant leaves are actual terminal wins,
since cutoff leaves have value zero.

A zero value is the minimax value of a finite game in which unresolved
cutoffs pay zero. Backward induction gives each player a strategy
preventing the other from obtaining a positive terminal payoff before the
cutoff. It does not determine what occurs beyond the cutoff.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Recorded computational observation.} The corrected implementation
reported
\[
 \Val_{27}(S_\varnothing)=0.
\]
This is a logged software result, not a separate machine-checked formal
proof object. The depth-$28$ iteration did not finish. The final record
contains $726\,138\,880$ node visits including that unfinished iteration;
the completed depth-$27$ record contains $338\,098\,896$ cumulative visits.
Tests compare 1,000 short-horizon cases with a plain-array implementation
and 100 endgames with independent full-depth values. These tests increase
confidence in the implementation but do not constitute an opening solution.

\section{Certificate and search semantics}\label{sec:semantics}
\subsection{Strategy-tree certificates}
For a claimed winner, a strategy-tree certificate must retain at least one
legal prescribed move on that player's turns and \emph{every} legal move
on the opponent's turns. Every leaf must be an actual win for the claimed
winner. A verifier replays each edge under the rules and rejects omitted
opponent replies, illegal moves, and false terminal leaves.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:tree}
A finite strategy tree satisfying those conditions proves a forced win
at its root.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Induct upward from winning leaves. At the claimed winner's nodes, a
retained child supplies a winning move. At the opponent's nodes, every
legal move is retained and leads to a winning child. The root is therefore
won regardless of the opponent's choices.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof-number search and WDL bounds}
For the Boolean proposition ``$\Pone$ can force a win,'' $\Pone$ moves are
OR nodes and $\Ptwo$ moves are AND nodes. At a true terminal proposition
assign proof/disproof numbers $(0,\infty)$; at a false one assign
$(\infty,0)$. An unresolved frontier starts with $(1,1)$. The recurrences
are
\begin{align*}
 (p,d)_{\rm OR}&=\left(\min_i p_i,\ \sum_i d_i\right),\\
 (p,d)_{\rm AND}&=\left(\sum_i p_i,\ \min_i d_i\right).
\end{align*}
A zero proof number is a true proposition by the same OR/AND induction
as \cref{prop:tree}; a zero disproof number is a false proposition.
Positive numbers are search bookkeeping, not probabilities or percentages
complete. The recorded depth-first proof-number searches timed out with
both root numbers positive.

A WDL cache stores exact values or inequalities $\Val(S)\ge a$ and
$\Val(S)\le b$. Its Boolean interpretation must account for whose turn it
is. For example, $\Val(S)\le0$ disproves a $\Pone$ win when $\Pone$ is to
move, whereas $\Val(S)\ge0$ disproves it when $\Ptwo$ is to move. A draw is
also a false instance of ``$\Pone$ can force a win.'' The implementation
checks all 18 combinations of turn parity, bound type, and bound value
against the allowed outcomes.

\subsection[Hashing and cached bounds]{Hashing and compatible cached bounds}
The main board representation uses nine columns of ten bits, including a
header bit per column, so a position key needs up to 90 bits and is stored
in a 128-bit integer. A table bucket index is only a lookup aid. A retrieved
entry is accepted only after comparison of the complete normalized key.
Consequently an index collision can discard useful information but cannot
by itself justify substituting a different position's value.

For several valid bounds on the same key, define
\[
 \ell=\max\bigl(\{-1\}\cup\{\text{stored lower bounds}\}\bigr),\qquad
 u=\min\bigl(\{1\}\cup\{\text{stored upper bounds}\}\bigr).
\]
Necessarily $\ell\le\Val(S)\le u$. Thus $\ell=u$ supplies an exact value,
and $\ell>u$ detects incompatible claims. The final merger retained
$16\,650$ distinct keys, with $11\,051$ exact values and no detected bound
contradictions. Absence of contradictions is a consistency check, not an
independent correctness proof of every cached value. The normalized-key
and dead-stone reductions remain part of the trusted implementation.

\section[Evidence and research status]{Auditable evidence and research status}\label{sec:evidence}
The mathematical statements in \cref{sec:classification,sec:obstruction,sec:oracle}
are ordinary proofs under explicit hypotheses. The following finite checks
are supporting evidence and reproducible artifacts:
\begin{itemize}
\item Exhaustive replay of all $328\,125$ classified configurations for
five representative openings; the $525$ all-one-prefix cases have explicit
stored response matchings and counterattack histories.
\item Independent paired-state legality enumeration of all $105$ cell
matchings in each of two $3\times3$ cases, and all $135\,135$ in each of
one $3\times5$ and one $5\times3$ case. The accepted matchings agree exactly
with \cref{thm:structure} in those cases.
\item A self-contained manuscript audit also replays all $42\,525$ classified
configurations at height five and all $134\,400$ at height seven, across
five representative openings each. Every counterattack finishes by ply $16$.
These checks support the infinite-height-family argument in
\cref{thm:obstruction}; they do not replace its proof.
\item Twenty-one independently constructed midgame line-cover certificates,
including nine forced losses, on 100 independently solved endgames.
Negative tests reject malformed or unjustified certificates.
\item An eight-node endgame strategy certificate corresponding to
\cref{prop:endgame}, and the full-board draw in \cref{prop:draw}.
\item Exact opening searches, horizon search, and proof-number searches
whose unresolved outputs are preserved as unresolved.
\end{itemize}

The generalized exact engine is based on Steininger's published
solver~\cite{steininger}; its research copy and license are retained.
Tromp's reference page supplies historical context and board-size
results~\cite{tromp}. None of the cited standard-board results is used to
infer the $9\times9$ value. The elementary classification, its application,
and the line-cover arguments are supplied in full here; this note is not a
claim of peer review or proof-assistant verification.

\subsection[Development directions]{Development directions with exact proof obligations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Adaptive response systems.} Extend fixed matchings to states
with finitely many response modes. A valid certificate must specify the mode
transition after each legal opposing move and prove a preserved invariant.
The obstruction in \cref{thm:obstruction} does not exclude such systems.
\item \textbf{Stronger strategic leaves.} Extend
\cref{thm:cover} with other proved threat-control rules. An unverified
heuristic must not be returned as an exact leaf value.
\item \textbf{Theorem-guided opening certificates.} Search for a finite
opening tree closed under every opposing reply, whose leaves are actual
wins or independently verified strategic certificates. By
\cref{prop:tree}, such a tree would prove an opening win.
\item \textbf{Ultra-weak arguments.} A nonconstructive argument must still
establish the empty-board value. Odd area alone fails by
\cref{prop:draw}; failure of a restricted strategy class does not establish
the opponent's full-game win. No such opening argument is claimed here.
\end{enumerate}
